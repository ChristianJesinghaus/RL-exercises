\section{Motivation}
Intrinsic rewards can help in sparse-reward exploration, but prediction-error bonuses can fail on irrelevant stochastic observations. This failure is known as the \emph{noisy-TV problem}: random observations stay surprising, although they do not help to solve the task. I therefore study whether a simple RND variant based on learning progress can reduce this failure.

\textbf{Research question:}
\begin{quote}
Can learning-progress-gated RND avoid noisy-TV distraction while keeping the exploration benefit of intrinsic rewards in sparse-reward MiniGrid?
\end{quote}

Here, \emph{robustness} means that extrinsic performance and distractor avoidance do not collapse when irrelevant observation noise is added or increased.

\section{Related Topics}
The project is based on DQN~\citep{mnih-nature15}, count-based exploration~\citep{bellemare-nips16}, and Random Network Distillation (RND)~\citep{burda-iclr19}. The original idea was a comparison of DQN, Count, and RND in standard MiniGrid. Since this is close to tuning the intrinsic-reward coefficient $\beta$, I move the focus to a diagnostic setting.

Kayal et al.~\citep{kayal2025impact} already study intrinsic rewards in MiniGrid using metrics such as return, observation coverage, position coverage, entropy, and reward-discovery time. The new part here is the noisy-TV stress test and a simple learning-progress gate, inspired by work on noisy-TV failures and reducible vs. irreducible uncertainty~\citep{mavorparker2022noisytv,hou2025beyond}.

\section{Idea}
I will modify \texttt{MiniGrid-FourRooms-v0} by adding a noisy-TV distractor. The distractor changes an irrelevant observation feature randomly when observed or visited. It does not change transitions, rewards, goal position, or the shortest path to the goal.

\paragraph{Compared agents.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DQN}: extrinsic reward only, $\epsilon$-greedy exploration.
    \item \textbf{DQN+Count}: position/abstract-state count bonus.
    \item \textbf{DQN+RND}: standard prediction-error bonus.
    \item \textbf{DQN+LP-RND}: RND bonus gated by learning progress.
\end{enumerate}

For RND, $r'_t=r^{\mathrm{ext}}_t+\beta r^{\mathrm{RND}}_t$. For LP-RND, let $\bar e(s)$ be an exponential moving average of the RND prediction error for abstract state $s=(x,y,\mathrm{direction})$. The intrinsic bonus is
\begin{equation}
    r^{\mathrm{LP}}_t = \max(0, \bar e_{\mathrm{old}}(s_t)-\bar e_{\mathrm{new}}(s_t)).
\end{equation}
High but non-decreasing prediction error should therefore give little reward. My hypothesis is that RND is attracted to the noisy TV, while LP-RND spends fewer steps there and keeps higher extrinsic success.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Environment.}
Main task: \texttt{MiniGrid-FourRooms-v0}. Variants: clean FourRooms and noisy-TV FourRooms with noise intensity $p\in\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$. If time allows, I will also move the distractor to a second room to check whether the result depends on its location.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Task performance: extrinsic evaluation return, success rate, area under the success-rate curve, and steps until first/third reward discovery. Evaluation uses extrinsic reward only.

Exploration and failure-mode metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Position coverage:} $|\{(x,y)\}|/|\mathcal X_{\mathrm{reachable}}|$.
    \item \textbf{Abstract-state coverage:} unique $(x,y,\mathrm{direction})$ states.
    \item \textbf{Observation coverage:} hashes of full observations.
    \item \textbf{Noisy-TV attraction:} fraction of training steps in the distractor region.
    \item RND error and LP signal inside vs. outside the distractor region.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
I first run small pilots to check that the noisy-TV wrapper works and that standard RND is distracted. The main comparison uses 10 seeds, fixed DQN hyperparameters, and a fixed training budget. I will choose one $\beta$ from the pilot and keep it fixed for the main runs. At most one small $\beta$ sensitivity check is planned; the main ablation is clean vs. noisy and different noise intensities.
